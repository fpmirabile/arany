# Credential-store stalls in other harnesses

Retrieved: 2026-10-06  
Status: primary-source comparison and isolated synthetic diagnosis; no real account lookup, credential access, native-store mutation or model inference

## Finding

Other harnesses do not share one solution. Claude Code, Pi and OpenCode avoid the Linux Secret Service dependency by using credential files. Codex exposes an explicit backend choice and an automatic fallback mode. Gemini CLI addresses a non-responsive native store directly with a functional probe and a short observation deadline. These patterns do not establish the cause of Arany's recorded timeout, or prove that a timed-out write was rolled back.

## Why availability does not prove writability

Secret Service separates session establishment, collection lookup/creation, unlocking and item creation. A service can accept a session and return no matching items while having no default collection or no unlocked writable collection. Creation/unlocking can require a service-owned prompt whose completion depends on user interaction. A headless process may therefore reach a usable D-Bus service but stall later waiting for a prompt. This is a possible mechanism, not proof that Arany's observed stall took this path. [Service methods and collection aliases](https://specifications.freedesktop.org/secret-service/latest/org.freedesktop.Secret.Service.html), [collection/item creation](https://specifications.freedesktop.org/secret-service/latest/ch03.html).

Prompts belong to the client's D-Bus connection and become invalid on disconnect. The specification also describes a race between dismissal and completion. Consequently, killing a helper closes its connection and bounds the application's wait, but cannot prove an already accepted write or deletion never happened. [Prompt lifecycle and dismissal](https://specifications.freedesktop.org/secret-service/latest/prompts.html).

## Arany's recorded parent-deadline miss

One generic parent-deadline failure was recorded during the Unicode editing work while other build work was running. Its output was subsequently recovered from the long-running development execution log, in the 2026-10-03 17:34:41 UTC record. The relevant failure, with the process ID omitted, was:

```text
thread 'setup::stalled_credential_bus_does_not_strand_unconfigured_start' panicked at crates/arany-cli/tests/session_run/loopback.rs:44:9:
product exceeded parent deadline
```

The source captured five seconds later shows that the historical `wait_product` polled child exit under a ten-second deadline and called `wait_with_output` only after exit. Thus the recorded failure means the observed PTY process had not exited within that wait; it is not a Secret Service error and was not an output-EOF assertion. That observer has since been replaced by the bounded concurrent-drain owner in [process.rs](../../crates/arany/src/test_support/process.rs). Waiting for exit before draining can deadlock a child with piped output, but the first startup phase uses null output and the historical transcript does not identify which `wait_product` call failed. No helper start/reap, composer-recovery or load metadata was captured. Neither a pipe blockage nor scheduling contention can be assigned as the cause from this evidence. A later passing run cannot establish that the historical miss was caused by load, account locking, terminal input, or the credential helper.

The Linux owner at the time of this investigation, `setup::stalled_credential_bus_does_not_strand_unconfigured_start` in [setup.rs](../../crates/arany-cli/tests/session_run/setup.rs), deliberately accepted a Unix socket connection and never answered D-Bus authentication. It exercised four credential waits: first startup and three resumed/preparation cases, including an unlinked running executable. Its total duration was therefore approximately twenty seconds; that was not one twenty-second credential operation. The first phase had a seven-second outer guard, and the shared process observer had a ten-second exit/output-EOF deadline per capture. These bounds had different owners from the helper's five-second operation deadline.

Test: [`crates/arany-cli/tests/session_run/setup.rs::stalled_credential_bus_does_not_strand_unconfigured_start`](../../crates/arany-cli/tests/session_run/setup.rs).

A direct optimized helper reached `AUTH EXTERNAL` against a silent peer and exited when the peer closed; that invocation omitted the production supervisor. Isolated startup, stdin/timeout variations and controlled CPU contention did not reproduce the historical parent miss. They neither recreate its full host load nor establish a load-independent timing guarantee.

The direct-helper probe and PTY comparisons used private homes, synthetic slots, test-owned Unix sockets, bounded captures and parent kill/reap cleanup. They did not contact the user's Secret Service. The pinned [zbus authentication handshake](https://github.com/dbus2/zbus/blob/zbus-5.19.0/zbus/src/connection/handshake/client.rs) waits for the authentication reply before method calls; its [method-call timeout](https://docs.rs/zbus/5.19.0/zbus/connection/struct.Builder.html#method.method_timeout) does not bound that phase. This explains why the synthetic peer needs Arany's external supervisor, not why the historical observer missed its deadline.

The current owner has a different startup contract. Unconfigured startup asserts that no connection reaches the synthetic bus and that the composer opens without saving an empty conversation. Its three resumed cases select saved access, close the startup authorization peer to recover the composer, then leave the submission-time credential peer silent. They verify interruption feedback, continued editing of the retained draft, terminal restoration, unchanged closed replay with no accepted objective or Run, and eventual peer EOF. The replaced-image case still exercises the mapped executable after unlinking. The old four-wait measurement does not describe this current scenario or prove its timing.

The [credential contract](../specs/credentials.md#selected-item-authorization) also distinguishes the supervised two-minute interactive authorization wait from five-second routine reads, writes and deletion. A startup authorization exceeding five seconds is not, by itself, a regression. The [supervisor corpus](../../crates/arany-cli/src/accounts/credentials/keyring_helper.rs) owns status-only authorization, cancellation/reaping, human latency and uncertain write-timeout behavior; the Linux product owner supplies separate PTY and replay evidence. Neither synthetic owner proves real native-service rollback or identifies the historical cause.

The historical parent-deadline miss has no established cause and is not a demonstrated credential-timeout regression. It is not retained as an active follow-up. Passing current scenarios cannot establish why the older observer missed its deadline.

## Application to Arany

Keep the existing [supervised credential boundary](../../crates/arany-cli/src/accounts/credentials/keyring_helper.rs): a bounded same-binary helper with kill/reap on deadline is stronger lifecycle evidence than a promise race or an in-process synchronous keyring call. Do not replace it with the latter patterns or increase its timeout as a substitute for diagnosis.

The separate native-store release gate requires a fresh private bus, isolated synthetic store and independent synthetic client. Record only operation-stage metadata: service activation, session establishment, default alias, collection count/locked state, creation versus unlock prompt, and prompt completion. Compare Arany's helper with an independent client on the same isolated service before attributing failure to either side. The deliberately silent authentication peer cannot prove those later native stages. Never inspect the user's real wallet or credential payloads.

For user recovery, retain explicit informed file-backend selection before a backend is pinned. A pinned native-store timeout must preserve the pin and report read failure separately from uncertain write/delete completion. Automatically saving the same credential to a file after an uncertain native write could create two copies and weaken the existing contract. The comparison supplies implementation options; it does not authorize changing Arany's backend-consent behavior.

## Scope and limits

Public source was downloaded and inspected at the listed commit hashes; branches can move. Claude evidence is documentation-only. No upstream binaries were executed, native stores unlocked or queried, real credentials read, or provider calls made. This research changes no product behavior or acceptance contract. Arany's historical observer miss still requires failure-stage evidence to identify its cause.
